Tajweed Rules Guide

This edition incorporates specific color codes and precise harakaat to facilitate reading Dalail-ul-Khairaat strictly according to the rules of Tajweed. This ensures complete peace of mind that your recitation is 100% accurate and standard.

1. Madd-e-Laazim - Purple
Rule: If a Madd letter (Alif, Waw, Yaa) is immediately followed by an original sukoon (jazm) or tashdeed within the same word, Madd-e-Laazim applies.

Duration: It must be elongated for 3 to 5 Alifs. In the script, it is highlighted in Purple.
الضَّآلِّيْنَ (Tashdeed)
كَآفَّةً (Tashdeed)
آلْآنَ (Original Sukoon)
قٓ (Original Sukoon)
2. Madd-e-Muttasil - Purple
Rule: When a Madd letter is immediately followed by a Hamzah (ء) within the same word, Madd-e-Muttasil occurs.

Duration: It is mandatory to elongate this for 2 to 4 Alifs.
جَآءَ
مَآءً
شَآءَ
3. Madd-e-Munfasil - Purple
Rule: When a Madd letter is followed by a Hamzah that appears at the beginning of the very next word, Madd-e-Munfasil occurs.

Duration: It is also elongated for 2 to 4 Alifs.
لَآ إِلٰهَ إِلَّا اللّٰهُ
بِمَآ أَنْزَلْتَ
إِنَّآ أَعْطَيْنَاكَ
4. Madd-e-Sila Sughra
Rule: If the letter preceding Haa-e-Zameer (the pronoun 'Ha') has a vowel, and the following letter is neither saakin nor a Hamzah, it is called Madd-e-Sila Sughra. It is marked with standing vowels.

Duration: Elongated for exactly 1 Alif.
بِهٖ كَثِيْرًا
لَهٗ مَا
إِنَّهٗ كَانَ
5. Haa-e-Zameer Bila Sila
Rule: If the letter before or after Haa-e-Zameer carries a sukoon or tashdeed, the Madd-e-Sila rule is voided. The letter 'Ha' is then pronounced normally without any elongation.
مِنْهُ (Preceding Sukoon)
عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ (Preceding & Following Sukoon)
لَهُ الْمُلْكُ (Following Sukoon)
6. Madd-e-Sila Kabeer - Purple
Rule: If the letter preceding Haa-e-Zameer is mutaharrik and the first letter of the following word is a Hamzah, Madd-e-Sila Kabeer is applied. It is marked with a madd sign over the standing vowel.

Duration: Elongated for 2 to 4 Alifs.
وَّرَسُوْلُهٗٓ إِلٰى
أَهْلُهٗٓ أَحَدٌ
اٰيَاتِهٖٓ أَضَآءَتْ
7. Rule of "Anaa" (أَنَا)
Rule: In the word "Anaa" (meaning 'I'), the ending Alif remains silent during continuous recitation (wasl). It is pronounced exclusively when you stop (waqf) on the word.
أَنَا عَبْدُكَ
أَنَا رَبُّكُمْ
8. Zaa'id Yaa (Khada Zabar)
Rule: When a word ends with an extra 'Yaa' (Alif Maqsura) preceded by a fatha, and is not connected to a joining saakin or mushaddad letter, a standing fatha (khada zabar) is applied for flow.
عَلٰى كُلِّ
إِلٰى رَبِّهِمْ
عِيْسٰى رُوْحِكَ
9. Zaa'id Yaa with Wasl
Rule: If the extra 'Yaa' is immediately followed by a joining letter (saakin or mushaddad), the elongation is dropped. Hence, a normal fatha is used instead of a standing fatha.
عَلَى النَّبِيِّ
إِلَى السَّمَآءِ
مُنْتَهَى الْأَبَدِ
10. Idgham Ma'a Ghunnah - Blue
Rule: If Noon Saakin or Tanween is followed by any of the letters ي, ن, م, or و (Yanmoo), they merge and are pronounced with nasalization (ghunnah). Both letters are highlighted in Blue.
مِنْ وَّلَدِ
سِرَاجًا وَّهَّاجًا
قَوْلًا مِّنْ
11. Idgham Bila Ghunnah - Gray
Rule: If Noon Saakin or Tanween is followed by ل or ر, the noon sound is entirely dropped and merged into the following letter without any nasalization (ghunnah). This is indicated in Gray.
مِنْ رَّبِّهِمْ
غَفُوْرٌ رَّحِيْمٌ
خَيْرٌ لَّكُمْ
12. Izhaar e Mutlaq
Rule: If Noon Saakin is followed by و or ي within the exact same word, merging (idgham) does not happen. The noon is pronounced clearly. There are only four instances of this in the Quran.
دُنْيَا
بُنْيَانٌ
قِنْوَانٌ
13. Ikhfa - Blue
Rule: If Noon Saakin or Tanween is followed by any of the 15 Ikhfa letters (ت، ث، ج، د، ذ، ز، س، ش، ص، ض، ط، ظ، ف، ق، ك), the noon sound is hidden in the nasal cavity with a light ghunnah.
أَنْزَلْتَ
عِنْدَكَ
مِنْ قَبْلِ
14. Ikhfa-e-Shafawi - Blue
Rule: If Meem Saakin (مْ) is immediately followed by the letter 'ب', the Meem sound is concealed in the lips and pronounced with ghunnah. This is highlighted in Blue.
هُمْ بِارِزُوْنَ
تَرْمِيْهِمْ بِحِجَارَةٍ
يَعْتَصِمْ بِاللّٰهِ
15. Idgham-e-Shafawi - Blue
Rule: If Meem Saakin is followed by another 'م', the first Meem merges completely into the second. This creates a tashdeed on the second Meem and is read with ghunnah.
عَلَيْهِمْ مَّدَدًا
لَهُمْ مَّغْفِرَةٌ
إِلَيْهِمْ مَّرْجِعُكُمْ
16. Iqlaab - Blue
Rule: If Noon Saakin or Tanween is followed by 'ب', the noon sound converts into a Meem sound with ghunnah. This is indicated by a small superscript Meem (ۢ) in Red.
مِنْۢ بَعْدِ
أَنْۢبِيَآءِ
سَمِيْعٌۢ بَصِيْرٌ
17. Izhaar-e-Halqi
Rule: If Noon Saakin or Tanween is followed by any of the throat letters (ء، ه، ع، ح، غ، خ), ghunnah is strictly prohibited. The noon sound must be pronounced sharply and clearly.
مِنْ خَوْفٍ (Noon Saakin)
عَذَابٌ أَلِيْمٌ (Tanween)
عَلِيْمٌ حَكِيْمٌ (Tanween)
18. Noon-e-Qutni - Pink
Rule: If a word ending in Tanween is followed by a silent joining Alif (Hamzat-ul-Wasl), the hidden Noon Saakin of the Tanween is given a kasra (zer) to connect smoothly with the next word. It is represented by a Pink noon.
مُحَمَّدِ انِلنَّبِيِّ
قَوْمِ انِلَّذِيْنَ
فَضِيْلَةَ انِلَّذِيْ
19. Huroof e Qalqalah
Rule: When the letters ق، ط، ب، ج، د (Qutub Jadd) are saakin, they are pronounced with a slight echoing or bouncing effect from their articulation point.
يَقْبَلُ
أَبْصَارِهِمْ
تَجْعَلْ
20. Huroof e Musta'liya
Rule: The letters خ، ص، ض، غ، ط، ق، ظ are known as Huroof-e-Musta'liya. They are permanently pronounced with a full, heavy, and thick tone regardless of their vowels.
خَلَقْتَ
صِرَاطَ
طَهِّرْ
21. Raa Tafkheem (Heavy)
Rule: If the letter Raa carries a fatha or damma, or if it is saakin and preceded by a fatha or damma, it must be pronounced with a heavy, thick tone (Tafkheem). Highlighted in Red.
يَا رَحْمٰنُ (Mutaharrik Zabar)
غَفُوْرُكَ (Mutaharrik Pesh)
فِي الْأَرْضِ (Saakin, Preceding Zabar)
22. Raa Tarqeeq (Light)
Rule: If the letter Raa carries a kasra, or if it is saakin and preceded by an original kasra, it must be pronounced with a light, thin tone (Tarqeeq). Highlighted in Green.
اَلْكَرِيْمُ (Mutaharrik Kasra)
وَفِرْعَوْنَ (Saakin, Preceding Kasra)
وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ (Saakin, Preceding Kasra)
23. Lafz-e-Allah (Heavy)
Rule: If the letter immediately preceding the word "Allah" carries a fatha or damma, the Laam in "Allah" is pronounced heavily and fully. Highlighted in Red.
خَتَمَ اللّٰهُ عَلٰى (Preceding Zabar)
نَصْرُ اللّٰهِ وَالْفَتْحُ (Preceding Pesh)
24. Lafz-e-Allah (Light)
Rule: If the letter immediately preceding the word "Allah" carries a kasra, the Laam in "Allah" is pronounced lightly and softly. In the actual script, this is kept in the normal font without any specific color, but for the sake of explanation here, it is highlighted in Green.
بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيْمِ
فِيْ دِيْنِ اللّٰهِ
